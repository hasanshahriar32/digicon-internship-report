1.1

Objectives

• Compare the optical output power characteristics of an LED and a semiconductor LASER
under varying injection currents.
• Understand and determine the LASER threshold current (Ith ) and external differential
quantum efficiency.

1.5

Results and Generated Figures

Physical Interpretation: The LED optical power exhibits a direct linear increase with injection current from the origin. Conversely, the LASER optical power remains zero below the
threshold current (Ith = 25 mA) and rises sharply with a much higher slope efficiency above
threshold. The LED demonstrates a standard first-order low-pass frequency response, while the
LASER features a distinct resonant peak near its relaxation frequency (fr = 3 GHz).

1.6

Parameter Comparisons and Model Limitations

Parameter Changes: Varying Ith to 15, 25, and 35 mA shifts the onset of stimulated emission
on the P-I curves. Changing the damping factor ζ directly controls the peaking behavior of the
LASER resonance curve.
Model Limitations: Below-threshold spontaneous emission and thermal rollover effects at
high operating currents are excluded from this simplified model.

2.6

Parameter Changes: Adjusting MZM drive voltage amplitudes alters the modulation depth
and resulting extinction ratio. Increasing the EAM active length (L) enhances absorption,
decreasing output power levels.

optical insertion losses, semiconductor chirp, and fabrication variations are excluded.
